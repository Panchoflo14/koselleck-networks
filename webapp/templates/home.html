Did the Sattelzeit reorganize the vocabulary as a system?

Reinhart Koselleck argued that 1770-1830 was not just an era when many words changed meaning independently, but a moment when the conceptual vocabulary of the language reorganized together, as a system. This tool lets you explore the same underlying data - words and how closely they cluster with each other, period by period.

Track a word across periods

Type a word and see which group it belonged to, period by period, in one continuous strip - which group it moved into, when, and whether that move is a genuine change or a data gap. Click any period for that word's full neighbourhood. Start here - this is the primary view.

Look up a word

Type a word and a single period, get back a plain list: its closest neighbours by similarity, its group, and whether that group changed since the period before. Closer to looking a word up in a period dictionary or thesaurus than reading a timeline.

See the network

A word and its closest neighbours drawn as a force-directed network diagram. Visually rich, but testing with non-specialists found it harder to read at a glance than the timeline above - kept for browsing the raw network structure, not the primary way to see how a word's group changed.
