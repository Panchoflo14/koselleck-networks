Periods
The corpus is split into 20-year windows. Each window gets its own embedding, trained only on that window's texts. So a word's position in one period says nothing about its position in another - it's measured fresh, period by period, not carried over.
Edges
A line between two words means they were close in meaning that period, by cosine similarity. Each word is drawn linked to its 15 closest words, so the graph stays readable instead of connecting everything to everything.
Communities
An algorithm groups words into clusters based on how tightly linked they are to each other - in practice, a shared semantic field. It can group at different levels of detail, from a few broad clusters to many narrow ones; what you see here is one such level, and the Findings tab checks that the results hold up across several.
Word color
Color marks which cluster a word belongs to. The word you searched for is always shown in plain dark ink, with a colored ring instead, so it stays easy to read whichever cluster it's in. From one period to the next, each cluster is matched to its closest match in the period before - so a word keeping its color kept its neighbourhood, and a word changing color actually moved to a new one. This is easiest to see moving one period at a time rather than jumping far ahead.
The question
That individual words drift in meaning over time is already known. What this project tests is whether whole clusters reorganize together around the Sattelzeit, not just word by word - something you can't see by looking at one word at a time.
