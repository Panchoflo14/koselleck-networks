Koselleck Machine

Did the Sattelzeit reorganize the vocabulary as a system?

Reinhart Koselleck argued that 1770-1830 was not just an era when many words changed meaning independently, but a moment when the conceptual vocabulary of the language reorganized together, as a system. This tool lets you explore the same underlying data - words and how closely they cluster with each other, period by period.

Timeline

Track a word across periods

Type a word and see which community it belonged to, period by period, in one continuous strip - which community it moved into, when, and whether that move is a genuine change or a data gap. Click any period for that word's full neighbourhood. Start here - this is the primary view.

 Word search

Look up a word

Type a word and a single period, get back a plain list: its closest neighbours by similarity, its community, and whether that community changed since the period before. Closer to looking a word up in a period dictionary or thesaurus than reading a timeline.

 Graph explorer

See the network

A word and its closest neighbours drawn as a force-directed network diagram. Visually rich, but testing with non-specialists found it harder to read at a glance than the timeline above - kept for browsing the raw network structure, not the primary way to see how a word's community changed.
